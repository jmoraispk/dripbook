How Dripbook works

Pick a book and a pace. Every morning, the next short part arrives in your inbox, until you've read every word.

  1. Pick a book

    Start any book in the library, bring your own PDF, or ask for one we don't have yet.

  2. Choose your pace

    Five, ten or twenty minutes a day, at the hour you like. Change it whenever you want.

  3. Read each morning

    Every part ends at a natural break, never mid-scene. Want more? Each email links to the next part.

956 classics ready today, millions more in search

These books are in the public domain: their authors and translators died long enough ago that anyone may share them. We take each one from Project Gutenberg, remove the modern notes, and cut it into parts at chapter and scene breaks. You get the whole original, in order.

Search goes further. It also finds about 30,000 more English books on Project Gutenberg, which you can ask us to prepare, and more than 5 million old English texts scanned by the Internet Archive. Open a scan and we clean up its text in a few seconds, then you can start it. We open a scan only when it's out of US copyright and its text reads cleanly; otherwise you can ask for it.

Every book is free to start. Later, one subscription will open the whole library.

    Your own PDF, one part a day

    Got a PDF you keep meaning to read? Open it in our editor. It suggests a cut at every chapter; move the cuts, skip the pages you don't need, then send the lessons to your inbox, one each morning.

    The PDF opens in your browser, and only your lessons are sent to us: the text, and the pictures cut from the pages. Pictures arrive inside each email, right where they sit in the book. We keep it all to send your drip and delete it 30 days after the last part. Use PDFs you have the right to read this way. Privacy
